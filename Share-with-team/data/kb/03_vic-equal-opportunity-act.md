## CHUNK: EOA-6
source: Equal Opportunity Act 2010 (Vic)
section: s 6
title: Protected attributes under Victorian law
jurisdiction: Vic
topic: protected attributes; Victoria; discrimination
keywords: protected attributes, age, breastfeeding, disability, employment activity, gender identity, industrial activity, lawful sexual activity, marital status, parental status, carer status, physical features, political belief, pregnancy, race, nationality, ethnic origin, religious belief, sex, sexual orientation, personal association
url: https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/equal-opportunity-act-2010
accessed: 2026-10-20
---
Section 6 of the Equal Opportunity Act lists the attributes on which discrimination is prohibited in Victoria:
- age
- breastfeeding
- disability
- employment activity
- gender identity
- industrial activity
- lawful sexual activity
- marital status
- parental or carer status
- physical features
- political belief or activity
- pregnancy
- race, which includes colour, nationality, ethnicity and ethnic origin
- religious belief or activity
- sex
- sexual orientation
- personal association with a person who has, or is assumed to have, any of these attributes.

Two points differ from federal law. Victoria protects "physical features" (height, weight, size and other bodily characteristics). It also treats nationality as part of "race". A Victorian employer must comply with both Victorian and federal law.

## CHUNK: EOA-8-9
source: Equal Opportunity Act 2010 (Vic)
section: ss 8-9; VEOHRC Guideline: Recruitment s 1.1
title: Direct and indirect discrimination
jurisdiction: Vic
topic: direct discrimination; indirect discrimination; unreasonable requirements
keywords: direct discrimination, indirect discrimination, unfavourable treatment, requirement, condition, practice, unreasonable, disadvantage, neutral rule, proxy, motive irrelevant, assumed attribute
url: https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/equal-opportunity-act-2010
accessed: 2026-10-20
---
Direct discrimination (s 8) is treating, or proposing to treat, a person unfavourably because of an actual or assumed protected attribute. The VEOHRC guideline notes that no comparison with another person is needed and that motive is irrelevant. A decision can therefore be discriminatory even if nobody intended it to be.

Indirect discrimination (s 9) is imposing, or proposing to impose, a requirement, condition or practice that disadvantages, or is likely to disadvantage, people with a protected attribute and is not reasonable. The VEOHRC gives these examples:
- unnecessary physical tests;
- a written English test where written English is not needed for the job.

Practical meaning for screening: an automated rule that looks neutral can still be indirect discrimination if it disadvantages a protected group without a reasonable, job-related justification. Examples include penalising employment gaps, requiring "local" experience, and favouring continuous recent employment. Because the person who applies the rule does not have to intend harm, the employer remains responsible for what a screening tool does.

## CHUNK: EOA-16
source: Equal Opportunity Act 2010 (Vic)
section: s 16
title: Employers must not discriminate when deciding who is offered employment
jurisdiction: Vic
topic: hiring; offering employment; job applicants
keywords: offered employment, deciding who to hire, job offer, refuse to offer, deliberately not offering, terms of offer, applicant, shortlisting, selection, recruitment
url: https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/equal-opportunity-act-2010
accessed: 2026-10-20
---
Section 16 provides that an employer must not discriminate against a person:
(a) in deciding who should be offered employment;
(b) in the terms on which employment is offered;
(c) by refusing, or deliberately omitting, to offer employment; or
(d) by denying or limiting access to guidance programs, apprenticeship training programs or other occupational training or retraining programs.

Under the VEOHRC guideline, liability also extends to individuals involved in recruitment, including managers and recruitment consultants. A person who authorises or assists discrimination can be liable, and an employer can be vicariously liable for discrimination by staff or agents unless it took reasonable precautions to prevent it.

Practical meaning for screening: a decision not to shortlist is part of "deciding who should be offered employment". The employer is responsible for a screening tool it chooses to use, so it should keep records showing the reasons for each decision.

## CHUNK: EOA-20
source: Equal Opportunity Act 2010 (Vic)
section: s 20
title: Reasonable adjustments for applicants and employees with disability
jurisdiction: Vic
topic: disability; reasonable adjustments; applicants offered employment
keywords: reasonable adjustments, disability, adjustment request, genuine and reasonable requirements, accommodation, software, ramp, modified instructions, breaks, rehabilitation, accessible interview
url: https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/equal-opportunity-act-2010
accessed: 2026-10-20
---
Under section 20, if a person offered employment (or an employee) has a disability and needs adjustments to perform the genuine and reasonable requirements of the job, the employer must make reasonable adjustments. The duty does not apply if the person could not adequately perform those requirements even with the adjustments.

The Act's examples of adjustments are providing a ramp or specific software, modifying work instructions or manuals, allowing time off for rehabilitation or treatment, and allowing more frequent breaks.

To decide whether an adjustment is reasonable, the employer considers:
- the person's circumstances and the nature of their disability;
- the nature of the role and of the adjustment;
- the employer's financial circumstances and the size of the business;
- the effect on the workplace;
- the consequences of making, or not making, the adjustment.

The VEOHRC recruitment guideline says this duty applies during recruitment, for example to interview arrangements. It also advises asking every candidate whether they need assistance.

Practical meaning for screening: an adjustment request is information for the recruiter to act on, not a factor in scoring.

## CHUNK: EOA-107
source: Equal Opportunity Act 2010 (Vic)
section: s 107 (and s 108 exception); VEOHRC Guideline: Recruitment s 1.5.2
title: Do not request information that could be used to discriminate
jurisdiction: Vic
topic: requesting information; application forms; interview questions; data minimisation
keywords: request information, ask about, date of birth, age, photo, marital status, children, pregnancy, religion, nationality, health questions, application form, interview questions, personal details, irrelevant information
url: https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/equal-opportunity-act-2010
accessed: 2026-10-20
---
Section 107 prohibits requesting or requiring a person to supply information that could be used to discriminate against them. Section 108 creates an exception where the information is reasonably required for a purpose that does not involve discrimination. The VEOHRC guideline says the rule applies to verbal and written requests, application forms and interview questions. It also notes that informal conversation can breach the law if it seeks discriminatory information.

The guideline advises handling these topics with care:
- Disability: focus on how it affects the job and any adjustments needed, not on the diagnosis.
- Pregnancy and parental status: avoid questions unless there is a clear non-discriminatory reason.
- Age and cultural background: avoid assumptions based on stereotypes.

Practical meaning for screening: assessment notes and AI outputs should not repeat or comment on protected personal details that a candidate volunteers, such as age, marital status, children, religion or health. Including them creates a record that could later be used, or appear to have been used, to discriminate.

## CHUNK: EOA-ADS
source: Equal Opportunity Act 2010 (Vic)
section: s 182 (discriminatory advertising); VEOHRC Guideline: Recruitment ss 1.5.1, 3.5
title: Discriminatory job advertisements and selection criteria
jurisdiction: Vic
topic: job advertisements; selection criteria; discriminatory wording
keywords: job ad, advertisement, selection criteria, young, dynamic, energetic, mature, recent graduate, native English speaker, local experience, physically fit, digital native, culture fit, discriminatory wording, penalty
url: https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/equal-opportunity-act-2010
accessed: 2026-10-20
---
Under section 182, it is an offence to publish or display an advertisement, or to authorise one, that indicates an intention to discriminate. Penalties apply to both organisations and individuals.

The VEOHRC recruitment guideline says advertisements should focus on the skills and abilities the job requires. It warns that phrases such as "young, dynamic team" or "mature, experienced professional" signal an age preference and should be avoided.

When writing selection criteria, the guideline advises employers to:
- clarify the genuine requirements of the job first;
- avoid discriminatory language and irrelevant personal attributes;
- separate essential from desirable criteria and rank them by importance.

Examples of problem criteria in a payroll role:
- "native English speaker": race or national origin proxy;
- "young and energetic": age proxy;
- "recent graduate": age proxy;
- "must have Australian experience": national origin proxy, unless genuinely required;
- "physically fit": disability proxy, unless an inherent requirement with a stated reason.

Such criteria should be removed from scoring and referred to HR to amend the advertisement.
