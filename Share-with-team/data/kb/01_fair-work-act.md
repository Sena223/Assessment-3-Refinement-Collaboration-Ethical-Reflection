## CHUNK: FWA-351
source: Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth)
section: s 351(1)
title: Employers must not take adverse action against a prospective employee because of a protected attribute
jurisdiction: Cth
topic: discrimination; protected attributes; hiring decisions; general protections
keywords: adverse action, prospective employee, applicant, candidate, job seeker, refuse to employ, not hire, reject, screen out, shortlisting, race, colour, sex, sexual orientation, age, disability, marital status, family responsibilities, carer, pregnancy, religion, political opinion, national extraction, social origin, breastfeeding, gender identity, intersex status, family and domestic violence
url: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2009A00028/latest/text
accessed: 2026-10-20
---
Under section 351(1) of the Fair Work Act, an employer must not take adverse action against an employee or a prospective employee because of the person's race, colour, sex, sexual orientation, breastfeeding, gender identity, intersex status, age, physical or mental disability, marital status, family or carer's responsibilities, pregnancy, religion, political opinion, national extraction, social origin, or experience of family and domestic violence. It is a civil remedy provision, so a court can order penalties and compensation.

The protection applies before a person is employed. A job applicant is a "prospective employee", so the protection covers every screening, shortlisting and selection decision. If a protected attribute is one of the reasons for a decision not to progress an applicant, the decision can be unlawful even when other, lawful reasons also exist.

Practical meaning for screening: a score, ranking or recommendation must never depend on any of the attributes listed above, or on something standing in for them, such as a name suggesting ethnicity, a graduation year suggesting age, or a career break suggesting caring responsibilities.

## CHUNK: FWA-342
source: Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth)
section: s 342(1), table item 2
title: What counts as adverse action against a prospective employee
jurisdiction: Cth
topic: adverse action; prospective employee; refusing to employ; terms offered
keywords: adverse action, refuse to employ, not hiring, not shortlisted, rejected application, different terms, unfair terms, job offer, prospective employer, prospective employee
url: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2009A00028/latest/text
accessed: 2026-10-20
---
Section 342 defines "adverse action". For a prospective employer and a prospective employee (an applicant), adverse action means:
- refusing to employ the applicant; or
- discriminating against the applicant in the terms or conditions on which employment is offered.

Threatening or organising such action also counts. The Fair Work Ombudsman describes adverse action against a potential employee as "not hiring someone" and "offering a potential employee different and unfair terms and conditions for the job compared to other employees".

Practical meaning for screening: removing an applicant at the screening stage is a step towards refusing to employ them. That is why a recruiter must confirm every rejection and must be able to see the reasons for it. An automated tool that screens people out for an unlawful reason exposes the employer in the same way as a human who does so.

## CHUNK: FWA-351-EXC
source: Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth)
section: s 351(2)
title: Exceptions - inherent requirements of the position and action lawful under anti-discrimination law
jurisdiction: Cth
topic: exceptions; inherent requirements; necessary requirements of the job
keywords: inherent requirements, necessary requirements, genuine requirement of the job, essential duties, exception, lawful, justified requirement, physical requirement, licence, religious institution
url: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2009A00028/latest/text

---
Section 351(2) sets out when action that would otherwise breach s 351(1) is not unlawful:
- the action is not unlawful under the anti-discrimination law in force where it is taken;
- the action is taken because of the inherent requirements of the particular position; or
- a religious institution acts in good faith, for a staff member, to avoid injury to the religious susceptibilities of adherents of that religion.

Section 351(3) lists the anti-discrimination laws that count for this purpose. They include the federal Age, Disability, Racial and Sex Discrimination Acts and Victoria's Equal Opportunity Act 2010.

Practical meaning for screening: a criterion linked to a protected attribute can be used only if it is a genuine, inherent requirement of the role, meaning an essential part of the job and not a preference. A payroll role's inherent requirements are things like accurate payroll processing and record keeping. Age, being a "native English speaker" and being "young and energetic" are not inherent requirements of payroll work. Where it is unclear whether a requirement is inherent, the criterion should be flagged for HR to confirm, not applied automatically.
