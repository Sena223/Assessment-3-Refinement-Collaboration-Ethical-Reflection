## CHUNK: PH-FWA-351
source: Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) [placeholder paraphrase]
section: s 351
title: Adverse action against prospective employees because of a protected attribute
jurisdiction: Cth
topic: discrimination; protected attributes; hiring decisions
keywords: refuse to employ, applicant, candidate, prospective employee, age, sex, race, disability, pregnancy, family responsibilities, carer, religion, inherent requirements, reject, screening out
url: https://www.legislation.gov.au/
accessed: 2026-10-14
---
PLACEHOLDER PARAPHRASE: An employer must not take adverse action against a prospective employee because of attributes such as race, sex, age, physical or mental disability, marital status, family or carer's responsibilities, pregnancy, religion or national extraction. Refusing to employ someone is a form of adverse action. Action taken because of the inherent requirements of the particular position is an exception.

url: n/a (fictional company)
accessed: 2026-10-14
---
PLACEHOLDER POLICY: Gaps in employment must not be scored negatively and must not be used to infer age, health, family status or commitment. Assessors judge whether the candidate's skills are current for the essential criteria, using evidence in the application. Where currency is unclear, the assessor records "information missing" and the application goes to a recruiter rather than being rejected.

PLACEHOLDER POLICY: If an applicant discloses a disability or requests an adjustment, this must not reduce any criterion score. The assessor flags the application so that a recruiter can arrange adjustments for the interview process. Physical requirements are scored only if stated in the job ad as an inherent requirement with a reason.

PLACEHOLDER PARAPHRASE: An organisation should collect only personal information that is reasonably necessary for its functions. Sensitive information such as health information generally needs consent. Assessment notes should not record personal details that are irrelevant to the selection criteria.

PLACEHOLDER PARAPHRASE: Job advertisements should not state requirements that exclude people because of a protected attribute, either directly (e.g. an age range) or indirectly through proxies such as "young and energetic" or "native English speaker", unless the requirement is a genuine inherent requirement of the job.
