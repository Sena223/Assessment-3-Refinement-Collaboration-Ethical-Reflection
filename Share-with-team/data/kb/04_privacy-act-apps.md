## CHUNK: APP-APPLICANTS
source: Privacy Act 1988 (Cth); OAIC guidance on the employee records exemption
section: s 7B(3); OAIC Employee records exemption
title: The employee records exemption does not cover job applicants
jurisdiction: Cth
topic: privacy; job applicants; employee records exemption; resumes
keywords: privacy, job applicant, prospective employee, resume, CV, unsuccessful applicant, employee records exemption, personal information, recruitment records, Privacy Act applies
url: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/employee-records-exemption
accessed: 2026-10-20
---
The Privacy Act has an "employee records exemption": acts done by an employer in relation to a current or former employee's record, and directly related to the employment relationship, are exempt from the Australian Privacy Principles.

According to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC), the exemption "will not apply to the collection of personal information about prospective employees". Resumes, cover letters, screening notes and scores about job applicants are therefore covered by the APPs. This includes AI-generated assessments. Only once a person is hired do the records of their pre-employment checks become exempt employee records.

Practical meaning for screening: every resume processed by the workflow is personal information covered by the APPs. That includes resumes of unsuccessful applicants, whose records never become exempt. Collection, use, disclosure to third parties (including AI model providers), storage, security and retention must all comply with the APPs.

## CHUNK: APP-3

accessed: 2026-10-20
---
APP 3 controls when an organisation may collect personal information. Personal information may be collected only if it is reasonably necessary for the organisation's functions or activities; for recruitment, that means assessing the application against the role.

"Sensitive information" is a special category (s 6). It includes information about a person's:
- racial or ethnic origin
- political opinions
- religious beliefs
- trade union membership
- sexual orientation or practices
- criminal record
- health, including disability.

Under APP 3, sensitive information generally may be collected only with the person's consent and only if it is reasonably necessary.

Practical meaning for screening: if a resume contains sensitive information that the employer did not ask for, such as a health condition, religion or ethnicity, the screening process should not record, repeat or analyse it beyond what is needed. A disclosed adjustment need is the exception. It may be passed to the recruiter so the adjustment can be arranged.

accessed: 2026-10-20
---
APP 5 requires an organisation that collects personal information to take reasonable steps to tell the individual certain things. These include:
- who is collecting the information;
- the purpose of collection;
- who the information is usually disclosed to;
- whether it is likely to be sent overseas;
- how to access or correct it, or complain.

APP 6 allows personal information to be used or disclosed for the primary purpose for which it was collected, here assessing the job application. A secondary purpose is allowed only if an exception applies, such as consent or a related purpose the person would reasonably expect.

Practical meaning for screening:
- Applicants should be told, in the job ad or an application privacy notice, that an AI-assisted tool helps screen applications and that a recruiter makes the final decision.
- Resumes should not be reused for other purposes, such as training models or marketing, without consent.
- Sending resumes to an external AI provider is a disclosure. The provider's location and data handling therefore need to be known. APP 8 also applies if information goes overseas.

## CHUNK: APP-11-12
source: Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), Schedule 1
section: APP 10 (quality); APP 11 (security and destruction); APP 12-13 (access and correction)
title: Keep applicant data secure, accurate, and destroy it when no longer needed
jurisdiction: Cth
topic: privacy; security; retention; destruction; access; correction; logs
keywords: security, protect, misuse, unauthorised access, retention, destroy, de-identify, delete, logs, run log, storage, access request, correction, accuracy, data breach
url: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-quick-reference
accessed: 2026-10-20
---
APP 10 requires reasonable steps to make sure personal information is accurate, up to date and complete. APP 11 requires reasonable steps to protect personal information from misuse, interference, loss, and unauthorised access, modification or disclosure. It also requires the information to be destroyed or de-identified once it is no longer needed for any permitted purpose, unless the law requires it to be kept. APP 12 gives individuals a right to access the personal information held about them, and APP 13 a right to have it corrected.

Practical meaning for screening:
- Run logs, scores and AI outputs about applicants are personal information. They need access controls and a retention period, after which they are deleted.
- An applicant may ask to see what is held about them, which can include an AI assessment, so assessments should be accurate and professionally worded.
- Factual errors made by the AI, such as a misquoted qualification, are an accuracy issue under APP 10. They must be corrected before the assessment is relied on.
